When the JMS provider reports an error on the connection of a listener, through the connection's `ExceptionListener`, each consumer of that connection checks its session by looking up its destination. A consumer whose session no longer works closes its session and `MessageConsumer` and creates them again on the same connection, with the same message selector and subscription, so that a durable or shared subscription resumes where it stopped. Errors that leave the session usable, such as the rejected asynchronous send that ActiveMQ "Classic" reports while its connection still works, don't recreate anything. This is what lets a listener of Oracle AQ recover after its database session is disconnected: AQ reports `JMS-120: Dequeue failed` and, on each later ping, `JMS-112: Connection is invalid`, but a new session on the same connection works. If a session or consumer can't be created, the consumer tries again after `micronaut.jms.listener-recovery.initial-delay` (1 second by default), doubling the wait after each attempt up to `micronaut.jms.listener-recovery.max-delay` (1 minute), and gives up after `micronaut.jms.listener-recovery.max-attempts` (10) attempts until the provider reports another error. Each failure, retry and recovery is logged by `io.micronaut.jms.listener.JMSListenerRegistry`, and `micronaut.jms.listener-recovery.enabled: false` turns recovery off. A non-durable subscription loses the messages published while its consumer is being recreated, and a connection that fails as a whole, for example when the broker restarts without a failover transport, isn't replaced.
